The symbols — the working whiteboard calls them icons, the same family — are
nature-drawn marks: leaves, blooms, shells, fronds. They carry the brand's
values in miniature. Eight of them have defined meanings in the style guide;
the rest are decorative artwork from the same family.

## Usage

From the icons whiteboard:

> Icons can be overlaid on top of any layout, they can become compositional
> elements, background elements, or even bullet points

### On the website

Value icons with labels — each symbol paired with the value it stands for.
See the [Website Guide](/website-guide/).

<PhotoSet layout="single" label="Website — six value icons with labels">
	<Image src={usageWebsite} alt="Website value-icon row on a panel background: the heart-bloom flower for fair trade and ethical sourcing, the ylang-ylang for environmental sustainability, the curling vine for handmade small-batch production, the coffee-bean cross for preserving traditional crafts and cultural identity, the orchid for empowering women, and the gingko leaf for doing something positive for the planet." />
</PhotoSet>

### On social media

Compositional and background elements — a symbol enlarged and tinted behind
the subject, or paired with a headline as a lockup.

### In email

Background elements and title lockups — pale transparent symbols behind
headers, and small icons paired with a season's colorway titles. See the
[Email Communication Guide](/email-communication-guide/).
